How is "near support" detected?

1. Pivot detection: A 5-bar fractal pivot low (bar i is the strict minimum of Low[i-2..i+2]) marks each "touch" candidate.

2. Clustering: Pivot lows within ±0.7% of each other are grouped into a single horizontal support level (greedy sort-then-walk clustering).

3. Minimum touches: A cluster needs ≥3 touches to qualify as a valid support level.

4. Proximity gate: The latest close must be within ±2% of the support level. Closes more than 1.5% below the support are treated as broken-support (filtered out — broken supports become resistance).

5. Two lookbacks: Each ticker is scanned over both 120 days (short-term) and 500 days (long-term). The longer lookback catches major structural supports (like the KALYANKJIL ₹350.95 example in your screenshot); the shorter catches recently-formed supports.

6. Sanity filters: Stocks with coefficient-of-variation of close <1% (e.g. suspended stocks like JETAIRWAYS) are skipped. Touch-density >30% of lookback bars is also rejected.

7. Score: score = touches − 0.3 × |Δ%|. Higher touches + closer proximity → higher score. Candidates are sorted by score descending.
